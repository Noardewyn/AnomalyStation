# 01. SS3D (RE:SS3D) and previous attempts at a 3D port of SS13

Research date: 2026-10-05. Confidence labels: high / medium / low. All URLs below were actually opened during the work.

## Summary

- SS3D started in early 2018 as a solo project by a developer under the nickname `*beep` (Unity). After a few months the author disappeared, leaving only concepts and assets (devblog 19-03), and most of the project's materials were deleted (About). ⚠️ The sources contain no direct statements about whether the source code was ever published; there is no code in the public GitHub before 2019-08-06. In autumn 2018 a team of volunteers revived the project (RE:SS3D). The public history of the GitHub repository RE-SS3D/SS3D begins on 2019-08-06.
- Over 7 years the project has remained in pre-alpha: the last release is 0.0.7.6 (April 2025), the last devblog is from April 2025, `develop` has only 710 commits, about 55 contributors over the whole history (⚠️ the figure from the GitHub UI was not reproduced; local git shows 61 unique author emails, some of them duplicates of the same people), only 6 commits in 2025, 15 in 2026 (as of early October). The project is alive, but at a minimal level.
- The main cause of the slowdown, which the developers themselves acknowledge: excessive and early scope (containers, atmos, electricity before there was a playable loop), then a full rework in 2022 that still has not brought back what was lost (FOV was ported only in August 2026, atmos is still not ported). Second cause: a shortage of programmers alongside an abundance of artists. Third: foundational systems (character animation) block everything else.
- In parallel there is an active fork `henkhooft/SS3D` (Unity 6 + URP, about 936 commits ahead of upstream, clearly AI-assisted development), in which interactions, health, combat, inventory, atmos (ECS) and electricity were rewritten and SS13 map import from `.dmm` was built. This is the most valuable source of practical lessons and requirements for us.
- Other 3D attempts (Primulus, Centration, UnitySS, Space Station: Source) ended in failure or never got beyond a tech demo. SpessVR (VR + desktop, closed source) has been in development for years and is still "coming soon to Early Access". The SS13 community has historically been skeptical of 3D and talks about a "curse" of remakes.

### F3. Stack (confidence: high)

- Unity: the README of early forks says 2019.2.0f1, in September 2021 it was 2019.4.30f1, now in `develop` it is 2021.3.15f1 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/develop/ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt). Render pipeline: Built-in (per the fork's FORK_STATUS), Input System 1.4.4, UniTask, Localization, FastScriptReload; EditMode/PlayMode tests, ParrelSync.
- Networking: Mirror (2019-2022), then FishNet (since late 2022; in `Assets/FishNet`, the dev guide describes FishNet RPC). The FAQ on the site still names Mirror, which is outdated (https://ss3d.space/faq/).
- Architecture: Actor / SubSystem / View / Event Bus / SubSystems Locator; one folder and asmdef per system (Audio, Chat, Combat, Crafting, Electricity, Entities, Examine, Furniture, Gamemodes, Health, Inputs, Interactions, Inventory, Lobby, PlayerControl, Roles, Rounds, Selection, Substances, Tile, Traits, Vision and others) (https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/tree/develop/Assets/Scripts/SS3D/Systems , https://ss3d.gitbook.io/dev-guide/llms.txt).
- Tilemap: hierarchy TileSystem, TileMap, TileChunk (16x16 per layer), TileObject, PlacedTileObject, PlacedItemObject; one object per layer per position (turf, plenum, pipes, wires, disposal, fixture); chunks are "barely used but potentially useful for networking and loading large maps" (⚠️ the claim about "spatial hashing for the network layer" was not found on the cited page and was removed; the layer names turf/plenum/pipes/wires/disposal were collected from other sources, and only Turf and Plenum are explicitly mentioned on this page); model adaptation to neighbors via MultiAdjacencyConnector (https://ss3d.gitbook.io/systems/tilemaps/technical-implementation.md).
- Licenses: code is MIT, assets are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial); Asset Store content is not used "for legal reasons" (https://ss3d.space/faq/).
- Documentation: GitBook (dev guide, design, systems, art guide) replaced Google Drive in 2023 (https://ss3d.space/devblog/23-03).

### F4. What is implemented and what is not, as of 2026 (confidence: medium, conclusion drawn from code structure, issues and roadmap)

Present (of varying maturity): lobby and rounds, roles and gamemodes (basic traitor), interactions with a radial menu, inventory and containers, clothing, health by body parts/organs with oxygen, stamina, ragdoll, examine, chat (restored in 2024), audio, electricity (SMES, cables, consumers), crafting (recipes, including walls), tilemap with a map editor and saving, FOV (ported only in 08.2026), localization, ghost.
Missing or blocked: atmosphere (issue #1464 "Resurrect the Atmospherics system" open since April 2025, estimated "rocket science", the old code lives in the `outdated` branch: https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/issues/1464), the character animation system (issue #1333 with a $90 bounty, and #1060 since 2023-03 is described as the foundation for health and combat: https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/issues/1333), full combat (issue #1246), airlocks/shuttles, chemistry, surgery, AI/cyborgs, most antagonists, admin panel (issue #1280), performance (issue #1373 to disable rendering of hidden tilemap layers; #1494 about 600 MB of RAM because the whole AssetDatabase is loaded at startup, https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/issues/1494).
Open milestones: 0.1.0 Finalize Rework (58%), 0.0.8 Character Rig & Animation (40%), 0.0.9 UI Guidelines (61%), without dates (https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/milestones). The roadmap in the dev guide puts MVP 0.1 (health, combat, crafting, clothing, shuttles), atmos and electricity only at 0.3 (https://ss3d.gitbook.io/dev-guide/roadmap.md).

### F5. Design decisions (confidence: high for camera/controls, medium for the rest)

- Camera: classic top-down, but angled, with rotation; no VR (https://ss3d.space/faq/). Controls: WASD (not click-to-move), 90-degree rotation, free rotation, tilt; interaction via a radial menu on holding RMB (https://ss3d.gitbook.io/design/basic-game-mechanics/controls.md). Mouse rotation was added in 2023.
- FOV: the principle is "the player sees only what the character could see", with materials divided into opaque and transparent (https://ss3d.gitbook.io/design/basic-game-mechanics/field-of-vision.md).
- Art: "retro-futurism", cartoonish and absurd in tone, but with pretensions of a detailed simulator; all models are made by hand in Blender, the tile grid equals the Blender grid cell, and 1x1 items are made slightly smaller than the tile so they do not get stuck in doors (https://ss3d.gitbook.io/design/readme/art-style.md , https://ss3d.gitbook.io/art-guide/3d/modeling/formatting/sizing.md). No automatic conversion of 2D sprites to 3D was found in the sources: models are made by hand, while SS13 maps (BoxStation, 2019) are imported by layout with automatic selection of model and orientation from neighboring tiles.
- Scale: walls were raised in December 2021 to fit mobs and machines, and the decision was called controversial (https://ss3d.space/devblog/21-12).
- Game design basis: a "simplified tgstation core" with inspiration from other codebases; server size 5-100+ players (https://ss3d.space/faq/ , https://ss3d.gitbook.io/design/readme/gameplay-summary.md). In the FAQ the authors estimate the time to SS13-level playability at a couple of years (the date the FAQ was written is unknown, but the project is now in its seventh year).
- Multiplayer: host/client and dedicated server. Server-authoritative character movement was still listed in the plans of milestone 0.0.8, and in 2026 the client-authoritative NetworkTransform is being disabled for items (https://ss3d.gitbook.io/dev-guide/roadmap.md , commits in the GitHub API). A separate Hub/CentCom (accounts and characters across servers) has been declared since 2020 (https://ss3d.space/devblog/20-01); the 2022 rework base included lobby, authentication and hub integration (https://ss3d.space/devblog/22-06).

### F6. Documented reasons for the slowdown and restarts (confidence: high for the quoted admissions, medium for the generalization)

1. Loss of a leader: `*beep` disappeared along with the code (2018). In 2020 "technical stagnation" and missing leaders are named directly (https://ss3d.space/devblog/20-01).
2. Shortage of programmers, repeated from 2019 to 2025 ("programmers needed"); the art departments move faster than the technical ones (https://ss3d.space/about/ , https://ss3d.space/devblog/19-09).
3. Overestimated scope and order of work: containers, atmos, power grids before a playable loop; no concrete design documents (https://ss3d.space/devblog/22-03).
4. The 2022 rework as a measure against bad structure, but it froze the core systems and stretched the return of features over years. FOV, chat, examine and lighting were still listed as "ports from develop" in milestone 0.1.0; FOV was brought back in 08.2026, atmos was not (https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/issues/1102).
5. Seasonal and personal reasons in the devblog (workload, "life elsewhere"), no paid core of the team (https://ss3d.space/devblog/20-09).
6. The bottlenecks "animation and atmos" are named directly (https://ss3d.space/devblog/25-03).
7. Lack of automation: in 2020 the automated build did not work and releases were done by hand (https://ss3d.space/devblog/20-05); the automated build was fixed and a workflow of autotests on every commit was added only in December 2021 (Em3rgency "fixed" the existing GitHub Actions setup; ⚠️ the word "appeared" in the earlier revision was imprecise: the build existed but was broken) (https://ss3d.space/devblog/21-12).
8. Split between the old and new branches: chat, FOV and atmos had to be dug out of the `outdated` branch (https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D/branches/all).

### F7. The `henkhooft/SS3D` fork: an AI-assisted push in 2026 (confidence: high for repository documents, medium for conclusions)

- 1699 commits in `develop` versus 710 in upstream; about 936 commits ahead; Unity 6000.3.16f1 + URP 17; last commit 2026-07-30, push 2026-08-19; 0 stars (https://github.com/henkhooft/SS3D , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/henkhooft/SS3D/develop/Documents/FORK_STATUS.md).
- The repository contains `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `.cursor/skills`, and FORK_STATUS mentions disabling StyleCop for the sake of AI development. This is an explicitly AI-assisted process in which design specs (⚠️ corrected: the "Design specs" table in FORK_STATUS has 20 rows, 27 distinct `design/*.md` files are mentioned in the text overall, and the `Documents/design/` directory actually has 39 files; the earlier "23" is not confirmed) and architecture notes serve as context for agents.
- What is done: interactions (three-level radial menu, UI Toolkit), a tilemap with an adjacency engine instead of recursive updates and AOI synchronization of tiles, health, combat (melee and hitscan), atmos on ECS with Burst jobs (O2, N2, CO2, plasma; in the foundation document a single flat z-level, liquids and phase transitions deferred, pipe networks marked in FORK_STATUS as partially done), electricity, areas, containers, chat, persistence, a map editor.
- SS13 map import: PR #71 (2026-07-30) parses `.dmm`, uses a YAML table mapping SS13 types to prefabs, and builds a "walkable shell" (floors, walls, doors, cables, pipes, disposal, lighting, APC); furniture, machines, items and door access are listed as "remaining" in the architecture document (that is, not imported yet; no deliberate rejection is stated in the source, so the earlier wording "deliberately" is removed; tables and SMES were added later); a Metastation v2 template is shipped (https://github.com/henkhooft/SS3D/pull/71 , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/henkhooft/SS3D/develop/Documents/architecture/2026-07_ss13-map-import-infrastructure.md).
- Documents with lessons: performance at Metastation scale (baseline measurement of about 58 ms per frame, ⚠️ this is a capture in the Unity Editor (Play Mode) from 2026-07-28 with the Map Editor closed, not a measurement of a release build: atmos and electricity simulation across the whole station, checks of every door in FixedUpdate, NetworkAnimator, rendering of hidden subfloor geometry; solutions: HashGrid indexes, AOI visibility, disabling hidden MeshRenderers, GPU instancing/SRP batcher) (`2026-07_metastation-scale-perf.md`); `Human.prefab` has grown to 15,604 lines and 126 components (`TECH_DEBT.md`); CI on EditMode only gives a false sense of safety; a render-less dedicated server crashes on render-dependent code, and host mode masks initialization-order bugs (`2026-07_headless-dedicated-server.md`); the "Boot storm" on disconnect and inconsistent "world readiness" were fixed with a session state machine and an `IWorldReady` graph (`2026-07_session-world-lifecycle.md`).
- Caveat: the fork is explicitly "not an official channel", there are no checks by the game community, and live gameplay is not confirmed by independent sources.

## Implications for the 3D port

1. Define the vertical slice before the systems. SS3D itself admitted that it built atmos, containers and electricity before a playable loop. We need a narrow slice (one map, 3-5 roles, one antagonist, the round loop) and only then deep simulations.
2. Do not allow a "rework with a freeze". The 2022 rework cost the project years: chat, FOV and atmos were brought back, or were awaited, until 2026. Our approach (a separate C# core without Godot plus a client) reduces this risk if the boundary (commands, snapshots, events) is fixed at an early stage and covered by tests. The network layer must be chosen once; replacing Mirror with FishNet was a major operation.
3. Animation and the character rig are blocker No. 1. The animation issue has been open since 2023 and blocks health, combat, sitting and ragdoll. Decide in advance: a single humanoid rig, layered animations (full body plus partial ones for the arms), held items and clothing as skins on the rig, IK, ragdoll, and a ready-made animation set (Mixamo is mentioned in their issue).
4. Design atmos and station scale as data in the core. The fork's experience: updating the whole station and every door every frame is expensive; we need "active-cell sleep", AOI synchronization, chunks, multimeshes or chunk meshes instead of a GameObject per tile, and separate visibility for subfloor layers (pipes, wires, disposal). In Godot the analogs are MultiMesh/chunk meshes and custom data structures in the core, not nodes per tile. Decide in advance whether a multi-level z is needed; in the fork atmos is single-layer.
5. A dedicated server without rendering from day one. The fork's lesson: server logic must not depend on the render pipeline; our Godot-independent simulator provides this for free, but tests must be run in separate processes right away (host mode masks initialization-order and late-join bugs).
6. Late join, world readiness, session lifecycle: build in an explicit session FSM, a world-readiness graph and buffered state for late clients; distinguish "unknown" from "confirmed empty" (the fork ran into "miss = space").
7. `.dmm` map import: SS3D did it twice (2019, 2026). We recommend: a DMM parser plus a type table in data (YAML/JSON), multi-pass processing (structure, then infrastructure, then items), and orientation heuristics for doors and wall-mounted objects. Decide in advance what counts as "comparable by parity" (station structure versus all items).
8. The art strategy determines the schedule no less than code does. SS3D made models by hand; in November 2019 about 11 artists posted on the order of 40+ new models in a month (⚠️ corrected: the earlier "more than 50" is not stated in devblog 19-11, the number was estimated from the list of items and is approximate), and according to the fork's asset index from the SS3D-Art repository about 2,045 assets are waiting to be imported, of which 672 are models (fork index, low confidence: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/henkhooft/SS3D/develop/Documents/art-asset-index.md). Compare with the number of objects in the tgstation clone; that is a task for other research directions. A choice is needed: a modular stylized low-poly model set (as in SS3D), or automated conversion from sprites (extrusion, voxels, billboards, 3D→2D the old-school way), or a hybrid. This is the key schedule risk.
9. A camera at an angle affects everything: wall height (in SS3D it was raised, "controversially"), wall cutaway and transparency, FOV, cursor picking (SS3D still has bugs of the kind "selection is offset from the cursor", issue #1515), a radial menu instead of a pixel click (my interpretation). Fix the camera, tile scale and wall height at the very beginning, before art production.
10. Do not repeat process mistakes: a leader disappearing, one person "carrying everything", the review queue, release instability. We need documentation (design spec, system map), automated CI and releases, and system owners. The fork's experience shows that AI-assisted development with "docs-first" speeds up progress (about 936 commits ahead of upstream; the timeline of accumulation is unknown, but the whole set of architecture notes is dated 2026-07), but it accumulates debt: a mega-prefab, gaps in Play Mode checks, UI fragmentation. The Godot analog would be huge scenes and monolithic scripts.
11. Licensing: SS3D assets are CC BY-NC-SA only, while the code is MIT. If commercialization or closed source is planned, SS3D assets cannot be used, while the code can. The fork's documents (design specs) look like a valuable source of requirements, but their license needs to be checked separately.
12. Manage community expectations: skepticism ("3D loses the charm", "all ports failed") recurs from 2012 through 2022. It is worth formulating in advance what exactly 3D provides (physics, visibility, immersion) and what minimum makes the port "recognizable".

## Open questions (what the user must decide)

1. What is a "port": parity with tgstation (thousands of items, dozens of roles) or a "simplified core" in the spirit of SS3D? What MVP (map, roles, antagonist), and by what date?
2. Camera and perspective: a fixed angled top-down with rotation (as in SS3D), a free third-person view, or first person? Art, FOV and the interface depend on this.
3. Art pipeline and budget: make models by hand, use SS3D assets (CC BY-NC-SA, non-commercial only), purchased packs, or automatic sprite conversion? Is there an artist or an art budget?
4. Commercial intentions and license: open source or closed, is monetization possible; this determines the admissibility of assets and code.
5. Team size and the role of AI assistants: solo plus agents (like the SS3D fork) or a team? This affects the discipline of documentation and tests.
6. Multi-level stations (z-levels), the depth of atmos (full LINDA-like or simplified) and of other simulations (chemistry, surgery, electricity): what goes into the MVP.
7. Multiplayer: how many players per server, are listen servers needed, is a global hub with accounts needed (in SS3D it has been declared since 2020, the 2022 rework base included authentication and a hub, current maturity is unknown).
8. Attitude toward RE:SS3D: ignore it, collaborate, or use the fork as a source of requirements and test data (Metastation import, design specs)?
9. Is import of real tgstation `.dmm` maps needed, and to what extent (station structure versus all contents)?

## Gaps

- For Primulus, UnitySS, Space Station: Source and Centration, wiki summaries and one 2014 news item were used, and no detailed post-mortems were found; Unitystation and SS14 were not studied in detail in this report.
