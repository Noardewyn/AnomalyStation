# 08. Licenses and legal questions of porting tgstation to 3D

Date: 2026-10-05. This is research, not legal advice. Base: a local shallow clone of tgstation (HEAD of 2026-10-04, no git history) and the primary sources I actually opened. All items where the conclusion depends on jurisdiction or interpretation are collected in the section "What to check with a lawyer".

## Summary

1. **Porting code DM -> C# is a modification of AGPLv3 code.** If the port "translates" tg code (even manually or with the help of an LLM), the result remains under AGPLv3, and for a network service §13 applies (offer the source to all remote users). Such code cannot be relicensed under MIT or closed: tgstation has no CLA, and the authors retain their rights.
2. **Mechanics and ideas are not protected; expression is.** The game rules can be implemented from scratch (clean-room), but texts, sprites, sounds, maps, lore descriptions, names and the "look and feel" must either be made by ourselves or taken under a compatible license. The SS14 path: fully rewritten code under MIT + assets under CC BY-SA 3.0 with per-file metadata.
3. **The tg statement "all assets are CC BY-SA 3.0" is unreliable in practice.** The sounds contain at least 28 recordings with non-commercial licenses, custom licenses (Zapsplat, Pixabay, Uppbeat, SoundFishing LESF), "assumed CC", music from other franchises, plus large bodies of files without attribution (1291 vox_fem files, ~880 instrument sample files: 349 ogg + 531 mid). None of the `.dmi` files (1448 in `icons/`, 1457 in the whole repository) has any license metadata at all. For any monetized release, tg assets must be considered "uncleared".
4. **Goonstation is off limits.** License CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; transferring code without a sublicense from the developers is prohibited. tg still contains Goon-derived pieces (wrestling, "Goon chems", hulk swing); they must not be pulled into the port without checking.
5. **Commercialization is possible, but only on a "clean" base.** AGPL and CC BY-SA allow selling. NC assets do not. Precedents: SS14 (MIT, Steam, free-to-play, Patreon), Unitystation (AGPL + CC BY-SA, Steam, Patreon). RE-SS3D chose art under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and thereby closed off monetization for itself.
6. **The BYOND ToS does not get in the port's way** as long as the port does not use BYOND software, the hub and authentication: the ToS is short and concerns only their service. Trademarks could not be checked (registries unavailable); part of the tg lore is under CC0.

## Findings

### A. Licenses of the tgstation code

**F1. Structure of the code licenses.** AGPLv3 for everything after commit 333c566 (2014-12-31), GPLv3 for earlier code. Separate parts: TGS DMAPI under MIT (`code/modules/tgs/LICENSE`), most of the tgui core under MIT (in `tgui/packages` there are 71 files with `@license`/SPDX headers: almost all MIT, one ISC; corrected during verification, was "49"), `tools/minibot` released into the public domain. Assets (icons, sound) are declared CC BY-SA 3.0 "unless otherwise stated". Source: local `README.md`, `tgui/README.md`, `tools/minibot/LICENCE-bot_folder.txt`, `.github/CONTRIBUTING.md`; commit https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/commit/333c566b88108de218d882840e61928a9b759d8f. Confidence: high.

**F2. The GPL -> AGPL switch: no CLA, the consent of all past authors is not documented.** PR #6689 was motivated by the "ASP loophole" of GPLv3: new code goes under AGPL, old GPLv3 code stays GPLv3, and a mix yields AGPL. ⚠️ The original wording "without the consent of all past authors" is not proven in the PR: in the discussion a participant asked whether the consent of all past contributors was needed, no direct answer was given; the maintainers said only that everything merged after the PR would be AGPL. What is established: CONTRIBUTING and the PR template contain neither a CLA nor a DCO (checked with local grep); `tgui/README.md` states explicitly that the authors retain copyright. Conclusion: you cannot relicense tg-derived code (per the GNU FAQ, only the copyright holders can change the license, and with several holders the consent of all is needed). Source: https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/pull/6689, local grep. Confidence: high (absence of CLA/DCO, motivation of the PR), low (whether the authors' consensus was obtained at the license switch), medium (conclusion).

**F3. Code translation and network use.** GNU FAQ: translating a program into another language is a modification, hence the same license. A modified version cannot be released under a different/proprietary license. AGPLv3 §13 requires offering the source of the modified version to everyone who interacts with it over a network; §5 requires licensing the whole work under AGPL. Selling copies is allowed. Sources: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html, https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html. Confidence: high.

**F4. The "one program / two programs" boundary.** Per the GNU FAQ, exchange via sockets and pipes usually means separate programs, but "intimate" exchange of data structures and dynamic linking make a combined work; a container/packaging does not matter. For us: if the Godot client and the C# core live in one process (single-player, listen-server), the client becomes part of the AGPL work. Source: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html. Confidence: high (the FSF rule), low (how courts would apply it to a specific architecture).

### B. What can be "rewritten from scratch" at all

**F5. Idea versus expression.** USA: 17 U.S.C. §102(b) excludes ideas, procedures, systems, methods of operation. Tetris Holding v. Xio (D.N.J., 2012): the rules and general mechanics are free, but the specific visual implementation and "look and feel" are protected; the clone lost. EU: CJEU SAS v WPL (C-406/10): the functionality of a program, the programming language and file formats are not protected; the expression (source code, documentation) is protected. Russia: Art. 1259 para. 5 of the Civil Code of the Russian Federation excludes ideas, concepts, methods, systems (⚠️ not confirmed by a source: on re-check, the consultant.ru page cited turned out to be the table of contents of the code without the article text; the wording is given from general knowledge, not from an open source). Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc., https://opensource.com/law/12/5/sas-v-wpl, https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_64629/. Confidence: high (USA, EU), medium (Russia, the page was read through a retelling).

**F6. The origin of SS13 itself is murky.** According to the BeeStation wiki, the source code was obtained by decompilation (player AZA and hobnob), then went to OpenSS13; Goonstation released r4407 in April 2010, and tg and Baystation branched off from it. Wikipedia words it more softly: the code was "released or leaked" in 2006. No Exadv1 license for the original is recorded anywhere. This is a systemic risk for all forks, not only for us, but the lawyer should know about it. Sources: https://nsv.beestation13.com/wiki/The_history_of_SS13, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Station_13. Confidence: medium.

### C. Goonstation

**F7. License and the ban on transfer.** All contributions to Goonstation are under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 (the tgui folder is under MIT). The README requires that code not be transferred into other codebases such as tg without an explicit sublicense from the developers (for example, under AGPLv3), and the same in the opposite direction. Source: https://github.com/goonstation/goonstation (and the raw README). Confidence: high (code), medium (Goon sprites and sounds, the README refers to them only indirectly).

**F8. What remains of Goon in tg.** `code/datums/martial/wrestling.dm` has a header: originally CC-BY-NC-SA 3.0, but "tg and derivative codebases" are granted the right to use it under AGPL, and the header is mandatory in derivative works. Without a header, but marked "ported from Goon": "Goon Chems" in `medicine_reagents.dm`, hulk swing in `code/datums/mutations/hulk.dm`. A lore reference in `TheDerelict.dm`. `Changelog.tsx` quotes an old Goon formula about rights for "SomethingAwful Goons". It is unclear whether a 3D port counts as a "derivative codebase" in the sense of the grant. Source: local grep. Confidence: high (facts), low (scope of the grant).

### D. tgstation assets

**F9. Attribution is missing where the most files are.** In `icons/` there are 1448 `.dmi` files without license/author fields (I parsed the zTXt metadata of all files: the keys are only state/dirs/frames/delay and similar). In `sound/` there are about 4.4k files (3793 ogg, 531 mid), about 90 attribution/credits/license files, but coverage is uneven: `sound/announcer` has 1378 sound files with a single attribution (in `vox_fem` 1291 files have no source: the word "vox" does not appear in the attribution files of `sound/`), and 880 files in `sound/runtime/instruments` (349 ogg + 531 mid, which is in fact all the `.mid` files in the repository) have no sources (licenses exist only for two synthesis_samples folders). Corrected during verification: was "1290" and "~700". Source: local scan. Confidence: high.

**F10. Non-commercial and custom licenses in sound.** At least 28 entries with CC BY-NC or "Attribution NonCommercial" (Freesound: ballistics handling, baton, helmet, gravgen, brap, human sniff, female cry/cough, ethereal hiss, roaring_fire, night_vision and others). The folder `sound/items/vacuum` is marked CC-BY-NC (source: Soundsnap free tier). `sound/music/antag/pirate/pirate_start.ogg` is under CC-BY-NC-SA-3.0 (taken from SS14, the record format resembles the RGA of SS14). Custom: Zapsplat standard (`jingle.ogg`), Pixabay Content License (reel, rattle), Uppbeat (throw), SoundFishing LESF (hiccup), CC Sampling+. Dubious: `title3.ogg` "assumed CC", `clownops.ogg` "taken from YouTube", "who knows where the original moth scream" without a source. Music from franchises: `title2.ogg` (a remix of the Robocop theme) and `elevator/robocop-short.ogg`. Besides that, Freesound's remix table prohibits re-releasing by-nc material under by and cc0, so the claim "everything is BY-SA" is internally contradictory. Sources: local `sound/**/attribution*.txt`, `sound/attributions.txt`, `sound/music/lobby_music/license.txt`; https://freesound.org/help/faq/. Confidence: high (presence), medium (legal qualification); the texts of the Zapsplat/Pixabay licenses were not opened (403).

**F11. Fonts and interface icons.** Grand9K Pixel CC BY 4.0; Pixellari, Tiny Unicode, VCR OSD Mono have only notes "the site says free for commercial" without a license text; Spess Font was created for tg; Font Awesome Free (icons CC BY 4.0, fonts SIL OFL, code MIT); tgfont icons from the Noun Project require attribution. Source: `interface/fonts/license.txt`, `html/font-awesome/css/all.min.css`, `tgui/packages/tgfont/icons/ATTRIBUTIONS.md`. Confidence: high.

**F12. Third-party IP inside the content.** The card game CAS (`code/modules/cards/deck/cas.dm`, `strings/cas_white.txt` 352 lines, `cas_black.txt` 144) is explicitly called a parody of Cards Against Humanity (the header indicates CC BY-NC-SA 2.0). Xenomorphs: `xenomorph` in 53 `.dm` files (129 files in the whole repo, including changelogs, maps, yml), `facehugger` in 14 `.dm` (51 in the whole repo), the design clearly references the Alien franchise (corrected during verification: was "61" and "16"). Isolated references: Warhammer (a weapon name in the arcade), Terminator, Metal Gear, Robocop. There are almost no real brands (surnames McDonald, Kellogg in name lists). Source: local grep. Confidence: high (presence), low (legal weight of the references).

**F13. Lore and names.** The repository `tgstation/common_core` (shared lore: Nanotrasen and others) is licensed CC0 (I checked the raw LICENSE). This is the only explicitly free layer of the setting. Trademark registrations for NANOTRASEN, SPACE STATION 13, SPACE STATION 3D could not be checked (the registries returned 403). Source: https://github.com/tgstation/common_core, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tgstation/common_core/master/LICENSE. Confidence: high (CC0), no data (trademarks).

### E. License compatibility and ShareAlike "contagion"

**F14. BY-SA 3.0 and code copyleft.** CC has no approved external compatible licenses for BY-SA 3.0. For BY-SA 4.0, GPLv3 is compatible, but only one way (adaptations can be licensed under GPLv3); AGPLv3 is not mentioned in the CC list. The FSF confirms: 4.0 is one-way compatible with GPLv3, and recommends not using CC for software. The practice of tg and Unitystation: code under AGPL, assets separately under BY-SA, with no attempt to mix them into one license package. Sources: https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/licensing-considerations/compatible-licenses/, https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.html, https://creativecommons.org/faq/. Confidence: high.

**F15. What triggers ShareAlike.** According to CC, SA is triggered by an adaptation, not by mere coexistence in one product. BY-SA 3.0 defines adaptation broadly (translation, any derivative work; synchronizing music with video is always an adaptation); an adaptation may be distributed only under the same license, a later version, or a compatible one. Hence (my conclusion): a 3D model made from a tg sprite is most likely an adaptation and must be released under BY-SA with attribution, while the game code remains a separate work. Sources: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/legalcode, https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/ShareAlike_interpretation. Confidence: high (license text), low (application to sprite -> 3D).

**F16. The DRM clause.** BY-SA 3.0 §4(a) prohibits imposing effective technical measures that restrict the recipient's rights; in 4.0 §2(a)(4) the licensor waives the right to prohibit circumvention of such measures. Consequence: do not encrypt or obfuscate asset packs with BY-SA content (including Godot PCK with encryption) and publish them openly. Compatibility with Steam DRM was not checked. Sources: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/legalcode, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode. Confidence: high (text), unknown (Steam).

**F17. Dependencies.** Per the FSF, Apache-2.0 is compatible with GPLv3 (not with GPLv2); Expat/MIT and CC0 are compatible with the GPL. Godot is under MIT: the license text must be available to the player (credits, a licenses menu, a file or a link); the Godot documentation only recommends `COPYRIGHT.txt` with the licenses of the engine's third-party components (⚠️ corrected during verification: was "must be attached"). Steamworks.NET (MIT) wraps the Valve SDK; I could not check the terms of the SDK itself. Sources: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.html, https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/about/complying_with_licenses.html, https://github.com/rlabrecque/Steamworks.NET. Confidence: high / medium.

### F. BYOND

**F18. BYOND ToS** (version dated March 4, 2015) governs the use of BYOND software and the website: conduct, membership, content removal. The copyright section urges making original games and promises to take down listings at the request of rights holders, but does not claim rights to your games and says nothing about a ban on porting or reverse engineering. Consequence: a port that does not use BYOND software, the hub, keys and accounts is not bound by the ToS. I did not find an EULA for the BYOND software itself. OpenDream (MIT) runs tg, Paradise and Goonstation on top of a C# implementation of DM; this is a separate, legally simpler path of "not porting but executing" (⚠️ the OpenDream README itself calls the project early-stage and expects bugs and missing features; maturity for a 3D port was not assessed, and executing the tg DM code itself remains under AGPL). Sources: https://www.byond.com/TOS, https://github.com/OpenDreamProject/OpenDream. Confidence: high (ToS text), medium (absence of other restrictions).

### G. Commercialization

**F19. What is allowed.** GPL FAQ: copies may be sold, the price is not limited (but the source must be available). CC BY-SA permits commercial use. CC BY-NC defines commercial as "primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation"; the FSF considers NC non-free; the CC FAQ says the boundaries of NC are judged by context. Any Steam release, paid cosmetics/subscriptions and, with caution, Patreon perks are best treated as commercial, and all NC assets should be cleaned out. Sources: https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/legalcode, https://creativecommons.org/faq/. Confidence: high (rules), medium (Patreon as "commercial").

**F20. Precedents on Steam.** OpenTTD (GPLv2) is distributed on Steam, GOG and the Microsoft Store. Unitystation: code under AGPLv3 (after 2017-03-12, GPLv3 before), assets CC BY-SA 3.0, Steam + Patreon. SS14: Steam Playtest since 2021, the developers promise it will be free after leaving Early Access, Patreon. Sources: https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD, https://github.com/unitystation/unitystation, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Station_14, https://spacestation14.com/about/about. Confidence: high.

### H. What SS14 and SS3D did

**F21. SS14: MIT code, CC BY-SA 3.0 assets, metadata for every file.** The code of the content repo is under MIT (Space Wizards Federation, 2017-2026). The license and copyright of each asset are in a metafile; the README says explicitly that some assets are CC-BY-NC-SA 3.0 and must be removed for commercial use. The RGA specification: `attributions.yml` in the folder with the files, fields `files`, `copyright`, `license` (SPDX or Custom), `source` (URL or PR). Example: `crowbar.rsi/meta.json` is marked CC-BY-SA-3.0 with a note that the design was taken from tgstation, meaning that porting tg sprites with attribution is legitimate. The forking guide requires reading the license of every file and warns about the copyleft of AGPL/MPL; the hub rule prohibits violating the licenses of other servers. The project was started in 2011 by developers connected to Goonstation. Sources: https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14, https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/master/LICENSE.TXT, https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/specifications/robust-generic-attribution.html, https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/master/Resources/Textures/Objects/Tools/crowbar.rsi/meta.json, https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/general-development/tips/forking.html, https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/community/space-wizards-hub-rules.html. Confidence: high.

**F22. RE-SS3D (Unity + Mirror): code MIT, all art CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, contributions are irrevocable, the Unity asset store is not used "for legal reasons". The project positions itself as a "simplified TG core plus the influence of other codebases". Conclusion (mine): NC art locks the project into non-commercial use, and if the models were derived from tg sprites under BY-SA 3.0, licensing them under BY-NC-SA 4.0 would add restrictions; I found no confirmation that they did so. Sources: https://github.com/RE-SS3D/SS3D, https://ss3d.space/faq/, https://ss3d.space/contribute/. Confidence: high (licenses), low (the conclusion about derivation).

**F23. Tracking tools.** REUSE (SPDX identifiers and copyright in every file, `REUSE.toml` for binaries, a linter) and RGA from SS14 solve the same task; tg so far uses only free-form txt files. Source: https://reuse.software/. Confidence: medium (applicability to our repo is my conclusion).

## Implications for the 3D port

**Choice of licensing model (the main decision, to be made before the first line of code).**

- **Path A, "open AGPLv3 port".** We translate tg mechanics and code (DM -> C#), the simulation core is a C# library under AGPLv3, the Godot client is also AGPLv3 (if it shares a process with the core), assets go in a separate BY-SA pack. Pros: legally the simplest, tg logic, strings and maps can be used directly. Cons: there will be no closed forks and no closed server modules derived from tg code; a source offer is needed (a "Source" button in the client, a public repo).
- **Path B, "clean-room".** We write the core from a behavior specification, not from the source (per F5 mechanics are free). Then the license is yours (MIT, proprietary, like SS14). Conditions: the implementers do not copy code and texts (LLM translation of the source is not clean-room), the specification is written by a different person than the one who implements it; all strings, names, descriptions, maps, sounds and models are either original or have a verified free license. More expensive and slower.
- **Path C, hybrid.** The core and game content are AGPLv3, while the services around them (accounts, payments, matchmaking, anti-fraud) are written separately and are not derived from tg, so they may be closed. This is a workable scheme for monetization if the boundary is drawn strictly at the network with no shared code.

**What requires what.**

- Do not copy anything from Goon (code, sprites, sounds) or from files with a Goon header; before translating, grep for "Goon", "originally licensed", "ported from".
- Import tg assets via an allowlist, not by default: import only what has a confirmed license (CC0, CC BY, BY-SA, public domain). Everything else goes to quarantine. For 3D models made from sprites, keep a reference to the source file and the author (the author is in the tg git history, but this clone has no history, so take it from the full repository).
- Classify assets by risk: (T1) everyday objects (crowbar, wrench) can be modeled from real references, the sprite serves only as a guide; (T2) unique tg designs (races, antagonists, unique items) should be considered an adaptation and released as BY-SA with attribution; (T3) everything that looks like someone else's IP (xenomorph, Robocop, CAS) should be replaced with your own.
- Sound is the easiest to replace: tg itself contains many CC0 recordings, and what is missing can be recorded or taken as CC0. Clean out at least the 28 NC recordings, custom licenses, vox_fem (voice-over of unknown origin), instrument samples, "assumed CC" and music from franchises.
- Set up a CI check: block on NC/ND/Custom/unknown license and on files without an entry in the manifest (RGA or REUSE). SS14 and the tg "separate txt" approach show what tracking turns into without automation.
- Do not encrypt or obfuscate packs with BY-SA content (F16). For Godot this means not enabling PCK encryption for assets.
- Ship the Godot license (MIT, mandatory) in the game, and preferably `COPYRIGHT.txt`, the licenses of .NET dependencies, and a Credits section with attribution of the tg authors and the repository.
- Names: do not use "tgstation", "/tg/", "Goonstation" as your own brand (attribution does not grant the right to the name; this is a general principle, I found no trademark policy for tg). The name "Space Station 3D" overlaps with SS3D and "Space Station 13/14", a clearance search is needed. For lore, it is easier to rely on the CC0 repository common_core and your own inventions.
- Keep your own team and contributors under a CLA or DCO from day one. For your own code this will preserve the possibility of dual licensing; tg-derived code cannot be freed this way.
- Authentication and player data: do not tie yourself to BYOND keys (a BYOND account is a service with its own ToS). Use your own account, Steam or OAuth, plus a privacy policy, GDPR/local laws (lawyer).

### Practical rules

**Allowed:**
1. Implement the rules, formulas, interactions and role structure of tg as ideas/mechanics (F5), provided that the text and visuals are yours.
2. Translate tg code into C# under AGPLv3, preserving copyrights and headers (Path A).
3. Adapt tg sprites into 3D under BY-SA (3.0 or later) with attribution, if the source is confirmed.
4. Sell the game, accept donations, sell cosmetics, if the assets are cleaned of NC and the source is available under AGPL.
5. Use the CC0 lore of common_core, CC0/CC BY sounds with correct attribution.
6. Release on Steam: OpenTTD, Unitystation and SS14 show that this is done.

**Not allowed:**
1. Transfer Goon code and assets without a written sublicense; the license is NC-SA.
2. Use any NC/ND, "assumed CC", YouTube clips, music and sounds from other franchises, custom licenses (Zapsplat, Pixabay, Uppbeat, LESF) without a separate review.
3. Relicense tg-derived code to MIT/closed (F2, F3).
4. Put AGPL code and BY-SA assets into "one license package" (BY-SA 3.0 is not compatible with the GPL family).
5. Release CAS and xenomorphs as is.
6. Use the names tgstation/Goonstation/BYOND as a brand, show "Made in BYOND".
7. Copy code from other forks without checking their license (Skyrat, Bay, Paradise and others appear in tg comments as "ported from").

**What to check with a lawyer:**
1. Whether translating DM -> C# with an LLM/manually/from a specification is a "derivative work" in your jurisdiction; where the clean-room boundary lies.
2. The scope of the Goon grant for wrestling.dm ("tg and derivative codebases"): whether it extends to the 3D port; whether to request written permission from Goon and the tg maintainers.
3. Sprite -> 3D as a CC BY-SA 3.0 adaptation: whether "the modeler looked at the sprite" is enough for adaptation status; whether an upgrade to BY-SA 4.0 is permissible.
4. Whether the attribution "tgstation authors + repository URL" is sufficient for icons without individual authors.
5. AGPL §13 for the architecture "server + Godot client + C# core": what counts as the modified version, where the network boundary lies, how to formalize the source offer.
6. Compatibility of AGPL with the terms of Steam/Steamworks SDK and paid platforms; the DRM clause of CC BY-SA under Steam distribution.
7. Commercialization (subscriptions, cosmetics, Patreon) in the presence of NC material, if some of the NC cannot be removed.
8. Trademarks and the name: clearance of NANOTRASEN, SPACE STATION 3D/13/14/SS3D, Syndicate in the target jurisdictions and classes.
9. The root of the chain of rights: decompiled SS13, Goon r4407 as the base of tg, the absence of a CLA.
10. Setting up a legal entity, GDPR/COPPA/local data laws, age ratings (tg has harsh and adult content; ⚠️ the claim "CONTRIBUTING prohibits Nazi symbols" was removed: on checking, nothing of the kind was found in `.github/CONTRIBUTING.md` or in the whole repo outside changelogs), responsibility for user content and moderation.

## Open questions (for the user)

1. Which licensing model is closer to you: an open AGPL port (Path A), clean-room with your own license (B), or a hybrid (C)? All other decisions depend on this.
2. What monetization do you plan: free + donations, Steam, cosmetics, a subscription, closed "premium" servers? Are you ready to publish the source of all versions that players see?
3. In which jurisdiction will development be conducted and who will be the legal entity/publisher (Steam, taxes, GDPR)?
4. Will the 3D models be built from tg sprites (then they are BY-SA adaptations) or entirely from scratch?
5. Do you allow forks and community servers, and do you accept that AGPL will force them to open their changes?
6. Do you want to approach the Goon developers and the tg maintainers for written permissions, or go without anything Goon-derived at all?
7. What do we do with the Alien style (xenomorphs), the Robocop music, CAS, and harsh content: replace right away or postpone?
8. Do we accept community contributions (CLA/DCO needed) or is a closed team making the first versions?
9. Name and brand: keep "Space Station 3D", use Nanotrasen, or build our own setting on top of the CC0 lore?
