First-Person /tg/station SS13 — Example UI Wireframes & Component Mockups
1. Choosing the Right Medium: Wireframes vs. Concept Images
When translating a system-heavy game like /tg/station into first-person 3D, keeping the live HUD minimal while tucking complex item management behind an overlay reduces visual fatigue over long rounds [1]. Using two stages of visual prototyping keeps engineering and art aligned:
Stage 1 — Functional Wireframes (This Document): Best for defining exact screen-space percentages, slot counts, contextual prompt syntax, and dual-inventory ergonomics so chat is never covered.
Stage 2 — Visual Concept Renders (Regular Chat with Canvas Off): Best for establishing material shaders, diegetic screen glow on handheld scanners, blood/frost screen vignettes, and industrial Nanotrasen styling. Section 7 provides tailored prompts you can paste into regular chat once Canvas is turned off.


2. Mockup 1: Live First-Person HUD (Closed Tab State)
When Tab is closed, the center 3D viewport stays clear so players can track threats, hull breaches, and projectiles [1]. Dynamic hover prompts surface context-sensitive verbs right next to the crosshair, while modern /tg/station Combat Mode replaces the legacy four-intent wheel with a binary state toggle [1][2]. Off-screen local speakers clamp to the left or right screen edges as directional pills so players still catch conversations happening behind them.

Key Interaction Callouts (Live HUD)
Zero-Percentage Color-Coded Health Doll: True to /tg/station, the HUD never exposes exact HP percentages. Instead, the segmented anatomical doll (Head, Chest, Groin, L-Arm, R-Arm, L-Leg, R-Leg) communicates injury severity strictly through color states—Green ([G] Unharmed), Yellow ([Y] Minor), Orange ([O] Moderate), Red ([R] Severe/Critical), and Dark Grey ([X] Missing/Prosthetic)—preserving the gameplay need for Health Analyzers and close inspection.
Active Hand Emphasis: [R-HAND ACTIVE] uses a high-contrast border (2px solid amber or cyan) and slightly enlarged slot dimensions (72x72 px vs. 48x48 px hotbar slots). Pressing X swaps the active border instantly to [L-HAND].
Physicalized Rig Binding (1 to 9): Slots 1 through 6 mirror items currently stored inside an equipped Toolbelt/Rig, while 7 through 9 map to Left Pocket, Right Pocket, and ID/PDA. Pressing 3 pulls the Screwdriver from the belt into the Active Hand; pressing 3 again returns it to the belt.
Keyword Pill Highlighting: Borrowing from Space Station 14 community patterns, character names ([Lucas]), job titles, and Syndicate codewords automatically render inside high-contrast pill badges in the chat box accompanied by a subtle audio tick [4].


3. Mockup 2: Three-Column Tab Overlay & Dual-Inventory Stripping
Pressing Tab unlocks the cursor and divides the 16:9 viewport into three non-overlapping vertical zones (0%--28%, 28%--64%, and 64%--100%). Docking both your open backpack and any external container or stripped player exclusively in the right column guarantees the full-height chat log on the left is never blocked.

Ergonomic & Interaction Rules (Tab Screen)
Universal Drag-and-Drop + Quick Transfer: Every item icon across Center and Right zones supports direct drag-and-drop. Holding Shift + LMB transfers an item immediately between your Active Hand, open Backpack, and the open External panel.
In-Slot Radial Timers for Stripping: Instead of spawning center-screen progress popups that block vision, stripping another crewmate displays a progress bar ([####--] 2.6s) directly inside the target's equipment slot. Moving beyond 1.8 m automatically closes the bottom-right External panel.
Nearby Floor Clutter Strip: Located at the very bottom-right, this strip lists small 3D objects within reach (scalpels, syringes, ID cards) so medics and engineers never have to pixel-hunt tiny meshes on tables.


4. Mockup 3: Real-Time "Anatomical Focus" (V.A.T.S.-Style Limb Selector)
Non-humanoid silhouettes in /tg/station—such as Xenomorphs, Space Carp, Golems, or Megafauna—make limb targeting ambiguous in real-time 3D. Holding Alt (or MMB) activates an immediate raycast sub-mesh overlay without pausing multiplayer gameplay.

How It Bridges Combat & Surgery
Sub-Mesh Fresnel Outline: Each anatomical group mapped on the entity's rig receives a translucent rim shader: Cyan for hovered limb, Amber for currently locked limb, and Red-Dashed if severed or damaged.
Unified Target State: Releasing Alt while aiming at [LOCOMOTION / HIND LEG] updates your middle-right HUD Health Doll target immediately. Subsequent melee swings, aimed shots, or surgical incisions with a scalpel automatically target that exact anatomical zone.


5. Mockup 4: 3-Tier Item, Radio & Machine UI Examples
In /tg/station, hundreds of machines communicate via TGUI by sending structured JSON state payloads from the server to a decoupled UI renderer [3]. Splitting 3D interfaces into three tiers prevents engineering bottlenecks while keeping urgent handheld tools immersive.
Tier 1: Diegetic 3D Handhelds (Health Analyzer & Bounced Radio)
Zero cursor-lock popups; rendered directly onto viewmodel screens or floating 3D world text:

Tier 2: Metadata-Driven Schema UI (65% of Station Machines)

6. Mockup 5: First-Person Grabs, Wrestling & Client Hallucinations
/tg/station combat depends heavily on grab escalation (Passive  Aggressive  Neck/Kill) and client-side hallucination hooks (hud_screw, screwy_health_doll, fake_alert, inhand_fake_item) [2][3]. Because first-person players cannot see their own sprite being grappled from above, edge brackets and client-authoritative UI overrides convey status without inducing motion sickness.
