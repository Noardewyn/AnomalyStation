# 07. Porting a 2D Top-Down Tile Game to 3D: Design and Art Pipeline

Research date: 2026-10-05. Context: a port of tgstation to 3D on Godot 4.7.2 (C#/.NET 10), with the simulation in a separate C# library. The figures for the codebase were obtained by reading a local clone of tgstation (commit 1d99bd7f, 2026-10-04), which has not changed. The confidence label refers to how reliable a statement is, not to how important it is. The report went through adversarial verification on 2026-10-05 (results at the end, section "Verification"); the ⚠️ mark means that the statement is not confirmed by the source or has been corrected.

## Summary

- The 3D attempts at SS13 (Primulus, Centration, SS3D, and others) got stuck not on "looking like SS13" but on "behaving like SS13". The most active analog, SS3D (Unity, since 2018), was still at version 0.0.7.x in April 2025 (0.0.7.6 from 2025-04-09 remains the latest release on the GitHub Releases page), and the MVP is planned after the character rig and animations are finished.
- The camera determines everything else: visibility, lighting, sound, interaction, the number of animations. It has to be chosen before development starts. The SS13 community is skeptical about first-person (because of the tone of the game); the SS3D project chose an angled top-down camera with rotation.
- The cost of the port is determined by the number of unique objects: tgstation has 1457 dmi files, 48,712 icon states, and about 11.4 thousand `/obj/item` definitions. A model for every type is unrealistic; we need archetypes, modular kits, and a manifest generated by a script.
- Direct conversion of sprites (billboard, voxelization) is cheap, but 76% of states are single-direction, so the other sides of an object would have to be "invented". AI generation yields unstructured meshes that have to be rebuilt by hand.
- A modular grid suits the world (Stationeers 2 m, Space Engineers 2.5 m, Godot GridMap default 2 m) plus free placement of items; 2D SS13 does not keep items strictly within tiles either.
- Lighting: by default Godot handles 512 clustered elements and about 88 shadow-casting sources per frame; we need a mix of baked light, emissive materials, and a small number of dynamic lights.
- Sound: Godot has no built-in occlusion. Audibility and visibility in SS13 are computed on the server (about 370 calls to view/viewers/hearers, about 535 including oview/oviewers/ohearers; ⚠️ the original figure of 527 was recounted) and must remain server logic independent of the camera.
- Successful 3D projects sharply limit their scope (Lethal Company: a solo author, low-poly; Alyx: physics deliberately cut down), while projects with a large scope take 7-9 years (Barotrauma 2014-2023, Ready or Not 2016-2023, SS3D since 2018).

## Findings

### A. Past attempts at a 3D port

**A1. List of failures.** The BeeStation wiki lists the 3D attempts: Space Station 13 in 3D (GLUT/C, 2011), Space Station: Source (never released), UnitySS (very short-lived), Primulus (Unity, abandoned after a failed Kickstarter), Centration (Unity, then Unreal, pulled from Steam in 2015, the Unreal version had no working multiplayer; the wiki gives the cause as "supposed technical limitations of the engine", ⚠️ the wording does not specify that Unity is the engine in question). The page confirms: Primulus was made by two Baystation12 developers, a tech demo was released, and the cause of the abandonment was the failed Kickstarter. ⚠️ The details "Primulus was top-down 3D, the tech demo included atmospherics and machine operation" and "Centration was first-person" come from search snippets and are not confirmed on the wiki page itself. Source: https://wiki.beestation13.com/view/List_of_remakes. Confidence: high for the list, medium for the causes (the wiki describes them sparingly).

**A2. SS3D: pace and bottlenecks.** The original was made by *beep in early 2018 (⚠️ "and vanished" is not stated on the About page: it says only "later revived as an open project"); the open revival lives on Unity. ⚠️ About speaks about volunteers more softly than originally written: contributor turnover is named as a general difficulty, not as "the main problem"; the two bottlenecks named in the devblog are animation and atmospherics. Devblog 2025 Q1 (2025-04-10, v0.0.7.6): progress is "slower than we would have liked". Roadmap: the "0.0 Rework" stage is still in progress, the active milestone 0.0.8 is the new rig and animations (walking, running, falling, sitting, ragdoll), while field of view, chat, examine, and lighting sit in "0.1.0 Finalize Rework" (⚠️ sound is partly done there, not "after"); the MVP (health, combat, crafting, construction, clothing, shuttles) is 0.1, atmospherics and electricity only 0.3. The networking layer is currently FishNet (dev-guide); the ss3d.space FAQ still names Mirror, i.e., it is outdated. There are no new releases after 2025-04-09 on GitHub Releases, i.e., about 18 months have passed without a release as of the report date (Discord activity was not checked). Sources: https://ss3d.space/about/, https://ss3d.space/devblog/25-03, https://ss3d.gitbook.io/dev-guide/roadmap.md, https://ss3d.gitbook.io/dev-guide/llms.txt. Confidence: high.

**A3. SS3D: what turned out to be expensive in the art pipeline.** From their Art Guide: every clothing item requires two models (rigged for wearing and unrigged for the hand or the ground, except headwear); hair requires shape keys so that it does not clip through helmets; the body under clothing is hidden because perfect weight painting is unattainable; a new humanoid species is best not made thicker than a human, otherwise the clothing will not fit, and it still needs its own animations; state variants (a broken lamp, construction stages) are kept in a single FBX with a shared origin; 1x1 items are made slightly smaller than a tile so they do not get stuck in doors. Sources: https://ss3d.gitbook.io/art-guide/3d/rigging/rig-formatting/equipped-and-held-items.md, .../body-clipping.md, .../humanoid-races.md, .../modeling/formatting/variants.md, .../sizing.md. Confidence: high.

**A4. SS3D: technical decisions worth remembering.** The tilemap separates "turf" (floor, walls, windows) from "fixture" (lamps, airlocks, tables), with prefab assembly and auto-rotation; interactions are event-based, both one-shot (pick up) and continuous (drawing with a crayon, automatic fire); camera: angled top-down with rotation, first-person and VR are not planned at the start; examine is solved by pre-assigning a color ID to objects instead of a real raycast, to remove cursor latency; ragdoll required four states (walking, ragdoll, bone reset, getting up); sound volume broke with camera zoom. Sources: https://ss3d.space/devblog/20-01/, https://ss3d.space/devblog/23-12, https://ss3d.space/faq/. Confidence: high.

**A5. SS13 community opinion on 3D.** A Goonstation forum thread (posts of 25-31 October 2016, i.e., ten-year-old data, 15 posts): first-person "doesn't feel right" for SS13, more suited to a realistic, grim game; participants leaned toward an isometric/three-quarter view; the thread author (Arborinus) proposed voxel mockups in MagicaVoxel and himself noted that voxels could be called "lazy", but that they are simple and do not require redoing the art completely (⚠️ this was earlier attributed to "the community"); another participant (somepotato) showed billboard sprites for characters in his own engine ("orange seams" were noticed); some participants considered the environment easy to build and the mechanics (above all atmospherics) hard to reproduce, while others argued about whether complex atmospherics are needed at all; a number of voices said that the 2D aesthetic is part of SS13's charm and that a 3D remake is doubtful. ⚠️ The claims about "walls blocking the view in top-down 3D", "gravity versus zero gravity" and "atmospherics in 3D being costlier on the CPU" were not found in this thread and are given as my own considerations, not as community opinion. Source: https://forum.ss13.co/showthread.php?tid=7358. Confidence: medium (forum opinions, small sample, 2016).

**A6. SpessVR: "feel, not parity".** SpessVR (VR and desktop) deliberately does not chase parity with the SS13 code, carrying over instead the aesthetic and the feel of the game; it warns about motion sickness. Status on the Steam page: "Coming Soon", Early Access planned, date not announced (the discrepancy with another site's snippet is resolved: the game has not yet been released on Steam). The page states directly that movement in VR is intentionally not smoothed, and advises VR newcomers to use desktop mode. Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/898200/Spess/. Confidence: medium.

**A7. SS14 as a counterexample.** SS14 "survived the curse" not by changing dimension: it stayed 2D, opened its own engine, and built a community; its design pillars: a dynamic environment, intuitive interconnected simulation, gameplay interaction between players. Sources: https://spacestation14.com/about/about, https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/space-station-14/core-design.html. Confidence: high. (WebFetch summarized the design-principles page too generically, so I do not use it.)

### B. Camera and its effect on gameplay

**B1. In the tg codebase itself, visibility already depends on direction.** There are defines for FOV cones (90/180/270 degrees), a separate `FIELD_OF_VISION_BLOCKER_PLANE` plane, and visibility and audibility in code are computed through `view()/viewers()/hearers()` (about 370 calls, about 535 including `oview/oviewers/ohearers`; ⚠️ it was 527). That is, first-person-like view restrictions exist in SS13 as optional effects, while the base view of "the whole screen of tiles" is guaranteed. Source: local files `code/__DEFINES/fov.dm`, `layers.dm`. Confidence: high.

**B2. Camera examples in the genre.** Stationeers: first-person with a "left/right hand" system (E swaps the active hand, LMB acts with the active hand, RMB toggles structure placement mode), per a wiki search snippet. Lethal Company: first-person, up to four players, proximity voice, two-handed items block other actions. SS3D: angled rotating top-down. SpessVR: VR plus desktop. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company, https://ss3d.space/faq/, https://stationeers-wiki.com/Beginner%27s_Guide/en (from a snippet, the page was not opened). Confidence: high (Lethal, SS3D), medium (Stationeers).

**B3. Social mechanics.** In first-person Lethal Company, proximity voice holds the social fabric together. The conclusion for SS13 (radio and chat will need to be supplemented with spatial voice) is mine. Confidence: medium.

### C. Grid, scale, z-levels

**C1. Typical grid sizes.** Stationeers: atmospherics is computed in 2x2x2 m cubes (8000 L of gas), mixing with six neighbors every half second. Space Engineers: large blocks 2.5 m, small 0.5 m (confirmed by the wiki); ⚠️ the claim "players complain about intermediate-size ships that cannot be built with either grid" is not confirmed by the wiki page (it came from a snippet), treat it as unverified. Godot GridMap: default cell_size 2 m, split into octants of 8 cells, only 24 orientations per cell, no individual transforms and no culling mask. Sources: https://devtrackers.gg/stationeers/p/ab534efb-the-airflow-update, https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_gridmap.html, https://spaceengineers.wiki.gg/wiki/Small_Grid (from a snippet). Confidence: high, high, medium.

**C2. Z-levels already exist in tg.** IceBox has 3 z-levels, Tram 2, Nebula 2, Meta 1; in the code, between 180 and 330 .dm files mention multiz/z_level/z-level (182 by `multiz|z_level`, 326 by an expanded case-insensitive pattern; ⚠️ "over 250" depended on the pattern); the number of z-levels is confirmed from the .dmm files; for 3D this means real floors and a mandatory decision on see-through views (transparent ceilings, cutaway). Source: local clone. Confidence: high. Specific cutaway techniques from other games were not researched (see Gaps).

**C3. Items are not bound to a tile.** About 3.2 thousand mentions of `pixel_x/y/z/w` in .dm files (of which about 2.2 thousand are assignments; ⚠️ it was "about 2400") show that 2D SS13 already positions things within a tile. In 3D this turns into placement points on surfaces (table, shelf) and physics. SS3D: a 1x1 object is slightly smaller than a tile (see A3). Confidence: high.

**C4. Tile map performance in Godot.** Godot documentation: occlusion culling via OccluderInstance3D (Embree CPU rasterization, baked only from MeshInstance3D; moving occluders is expensive, while hiding/showing is cheap, i.e., doors are a good fit); for large worlds it advises tile-based loading (this is written on the optimizing_3d_performance page, not on occlusion_culling); automatic instancing in Forward+ and MultiMesh for thousands of identical objects. Forum snippet: GridMap performance drops as the map grows, and MultiMesh draws all instances regardless of visibility if they are scattered far apart (⚠️ the official documentation says nothing about MultiMesh lacking culling; left as unverified). Sources: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/occlusion_culling.html, https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/performance/optimizing_3d_performance.html. Confidence: high (documentation), low (forum).

### D. Lighting and atmospherics

**D1. Light budgets in Godot.** Forward+: by default 512 clustered elements per frame (omni, spot, decal, reflection probe, configurable); Mobile: 8+8 per mesh, 256+256 per frame, not changeable; Compatibility: 8+8 per mesh; up to 8 DirectionalLight3D; the shadow atlas by default is sized for approximately 88 shadow-casting sources. Recommendations: baked light, emissive materials, distance fade. Source: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/lights_and_shadows.html. Confidence: high.

**D2. GI options.** VoxelGI is real-time and accounts for dynamic objects, but leaks through thin walls; SDFGI is semi-automatic, but has cascade shifts and an incomplete contribution from dynamic objects; LightmapGI is the highest quality but static; SSIL supplements them. For interiors, a disabled or constant ambient, SSAO, volumetric fog, and Filmic/Reinhard tonemapping are recommended; Forward+ supports all of it. 4.7 added AreaLight3D and nearest-neighbor scaling for a retro style. Sources: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/introduction_to_global_illumination.html, https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html, https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/. Confidence: high (documentation), medium (release notes summarized briefly).

**D3. How lighting works in tg.** Render planes: a lighting plane per tile, emissive and bloom masks, a specular mask, a separate unlit game plane; 252 `light_range` assignments; LIGHTING_CUTOFF and night vision thresholds. All of this consists of screen-space 2D mechanisms with no direct analog: a "dark tile" in 3D will have to be built from soft dynamic lights plus a minimal ambient, otherwise the game becomes unreadable. Source: local `code/__DEFINES/layers.dm`, `lighting.dm`. Confidence: high (code facts), medium (conclusion).

**D4. Dark interiors and style.** Lethal Company achieves its look through HDRP, low-resolution rendering, volumetrics, and post-processing (posterization, edge detection), and holds 100+ FPS on mid-range hardware. In 2023 SS3D complained that the lamps of the electrical system were poorly visible without proper world lighting. Sources: https://80.lv/articles/how-to-achieve-lethal-company-s-graphics-with-unity-hdrp, https://ss3d.space/devblog/23-12. Confidence: high.

**D5. Atmospherics (gas) in 3D.** Evacuating a 5x5x5 m room (down to 1 Pa) took 13 min 45 s in the old Stationeers model, because the last 10% of pressure was pumped out 200-400% longer than the first 90%; this was fixed with mixing scaled by the number of neighbors and linear interpolation by pressure difference (in typical large rooms the gain is about 150%, in the best case of an airlock up to 300%). ⚠️ Removed: "Stationeers moved atmospherics into separate threads" (the devtrackers page says nothing about threads) and "the bottlenecks as bases grew were lighting and rendering, not atmospherics" (the page contains nothing about lighting and rendering; the snippet was not confirmed). Source: https://devtrackers.gg/stationeers/p/ab534efb-the-airflow-update. Confidence: high (vacuum and mixing model).

### E. Interaction

**E1. SS3D experience.** An interaction wheel, an intent cycler, a body-part targeting UI (devblog 2020-08), an event-based interaction model with one-shot and continuous actions. Sources: https://ss3d.space/devblog/20-08/, https://ss3d.space/devblog/20-01/. Confidence: high.

**E2. VR game experience.** Alyx deliberately did not implement Boneworks-style physics: without force feedback, heavy physical objects feel empty. At GDC 2024 Greensky Games showed that sliders, handles, buttons, and hinges can be built through physical constraints as modular interaction types. Sources: https://uploadvr.com/half-life-alyx-boneworks-physics/, https://gdcvault.com/play/1034486/Future-Realities-Summit-Using-Physics. Confidence: high. Conclusion: a set of interaction "archetypes" is cheaper than a unique implementation for each item (the interpretation is mine).

**E3. Barotrauma and UI.** Barotrauma's lead developer was inspired by SS13 and wanted to fix its "unintuitive interface"; the game is 2D (MonoGame), in development since 2014, 1.0 in March 2023. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barotrauma_(video_game). Confidence: high.

**E4. Ready or Not.** A context menu of commands, door breaching, leaning, and a fiberscope; Unreal Engine 5; a project since 2016, 1.0 release in December 2023. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_or_Not_(video_game). Confidence: high.

**E5. Garry's Mod.** The basis of its long life: Lua-scriptable game modes (TTT, Prop Hunt, DarkRP, Spacebuild, Wiremod) and a physics "gun" for manipulating props. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry%27s_Mod. Confidence: high. Conclusion: the data and scripts of items and roles must be external and moddable.

### F. Animations and customization

**F1. Reusing animations in Godot.** Retargeting via BoneMap and SkeletonProfileHumanoid: identical bone names and hierarchy are required, with aligned rest poses (T-pose, +Z); the Overwrite Axis flag can give poor results when the rest pose is set externally. Source: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html. Confidence: high.

**F2. Free animations.** Quaternius Universal Animation Library: more than 120 animations (locomotion in 8 directions, pushing, crawling, sitting, weapon, emotes, death), a universal rig, CC0. Mixamo: could not check the terms (403). Source: https://quaternius.com/packs/universalanimationlibrary.html. Confidence: high.

**F3. Scale of customization in tg.** About 1.8 thousand clothing types, about 3.5 thousand on-mob clothing states, about 3.4 thousand inhand sprite states (left/right hand), 341 human face states, 1332 states in `icons/mob/human`. Inhand sprites are replaced in 3D by attachment points and hand poses. Source: local count. Confidence: medium (count by regexp).

### G. Sound

**G1.** The tg `sound/` directory has 4423 files (121 MB), but ⚠️ only about 3.8 thousand of them are sound files (3793 ogg and 1 mp3), another 531 are `.mid` (MIDI instrument sets), and the rest are auxiliary txt/md/mod files; about 3.7 thousand `playsound` calls (3727); the sounds are licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 (see below). Godot has AudioStreamPlayer3D and reverb zones, but no ready-made occlusion: there are the addons SpatialAudio3D (raycast, geometry-based reverb, filtering behind a wall) and Raytraced Audio. Sources: local clone; https://gamefromscratch.com/godot-raytraced-audio/, Godot Asset Library (from a snippet). Confidence: high (counts), medium (the addons were not tested).

**G2.** In 2023 SS3D redid the sounds for interaction, collisions, ambient, and music with network synchronization. My conclusion: occlusion and "muffling in vacuum" (by pressure from the atmospherics grid) will become a noticeable part of the work. Confidence: medium.

### H. Art pipeline: approaches and cost

**Volume data (local tgstation, counted by a script over DMI metadata).** 1457 dmi files, of which 1236 are 32x32; 48,712 states; 36,993 single-direction, 11,472 four-direction, 247 eight-direction; 5687 animated; total frames (directions x frames) 138,919. The largest groups: smooth_structures 4234 states (smoothing bitmasks for walls, tables, windows), on-mob clothing 3551, inhands 3449, floors 3172, walls 2035, machines 2508, pipes and cables 2012. Typepaths (strict recount: unique definitions at the start of a line with no body on the same line): `/obj/item` about 11.4 thousand (11,390), `/obj/machinery` about 1.7 thousand (1666), `/obj/structure` about 2.1 thousand (2137), `/turf` 848, `/mob/living` 654 (⚠️ the original 11.5 thousand, 2.2 thousand, 863 and 666 were overstated by 1-3%); `/obj/item/clothing` 1765. MetaStation uses about 2.5 thousand unique obj/turf/mob typepaths in the map (2532; 2703 with areas). All the DMI numbers (1457, 1236, 48,712, 36,993, 11,472, 247, 5687, 138,919 and the breakdown by directory) were recounted by an independent script and matched exactly; the share of single-direction states is 75.9%. Confidence: medium (the method is crude, inheritance is not accounted for, but the order of magnitude is reliable).

**H1. Billboard/2.5D from DMI.** The cost is minimal, and all 1457 files are used as is. Godot Sprite3D with billboard mode; there is a CC0 shader for multi-directional sprites in the Doom style (it picks the frame by angle, mirrors the sides, and has a known problem with a strictly top-down view). Downsides: a lit billboard gives away the flat plane, so normal maps or workarounds are needed (Godot 3D nodes have no light masks as in 2D); transparency is sorted by node position, not by vertices, and is fixed with alpha scissor/hash; 76% of states are single-direction, so objects have no "side view". Precedent: HD-2D in Octopath Traveler (2D sprites in lit 3D; the Unreal page could not be opened, the information is from a snippet). Sources: https://godotshaders.com/shader/multi-directional-sprite-shader/, https://forum.godotengine.org/t/lighting-on-a-billboard/7409, https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/3d_rendering_limitations.html, https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_spritebase3d.html. Confidence: high (Godot), medium (HD-2D).

**H2. Voxelization and sprite stacking.** There are tools (AutoVoxel for Unity, Voxy for GameMaker, Pix 2D to 3D with OBJ export) that turn pixels into voxels in seconds. But a top-down sprite has no depth data or side views; the result is a "stretched picture" (my conclusion). Sprite stacking gives fewer polygons but more overdraw. Sources: search snippets (the tool pages were not opened) and the SS13 thread from A5. Confidence: low-medium.

**H3. Kitbash from CC0 kits.** Quaternius Modular Sci-Fi MegaKit: more than 270 modular parts for a grid (the free tier has 190), CC0, FBX/OBJ/glTF formats; Kenney Space Station Kit: 90 models, CC0; Kenney Modular Space Kit: 40 tiles (from a snippet). They cover walls, floors, doors, columns, and some props, but not machines and items specific to SS13. Sources: https://quaternius.itch.io/modular-sci-fi-megakit, https://www.kenney.nl/assets/space-station-kit. Confidence: high.

**H4. Manual modeling.** The estimates are weak: a vendor FAQ (snippet only) gives $50-150 for a simple prop and $200-500 for a detailed one; the ufo3d test names a rate of about $20/hour and $80-200 for rebuilding a model. My rough estimate at 300-500 unique meshes: $30-150 thousand for hiring, or months of in-house work; the volunteer SS3D project has still not completed the basic set after years. Source: https://ufo3d.com/?p=11629. Confidence: low.

**H5. AI generation.** A test of four generators (Meshy 7, Hunyuan3D V3, Tripo V3.1, and one more): the best (Meshy, about 3 million polygons) still produces a "solid welded shell" without logical structure, unsuitable for texturing and variants; manual retopology is needed after that. Hunyuan3D-2 (open weights) requires about 6 GB of VRAM for shape and 16 GB with texture, and its license does not apply in the territory of the EU, the UK, and South Korea, restricts use above 1 million MAU, and forbids using the outputs to improve other AI models. TRELLIS: an MIT license for the models and most of the code (⚠️ some submodules are under other licenses), the code was tested only on Linux (Windows is not officially supported, rather than "impossible") and requires an NVIDIA GPU with 16 GB or more. Note: the ufo3d test examined Hunyuan 3D V3, while the license restrictions listed here belong to the Hunyuan3D-2.0 license; the terms for V3 were not checked. Meshy: the free plan gives CC BY 4.0 with attribution, paid subscribers own the results. Sources: https://ufo3d.com/?p=11629, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2/main/LICENSE, https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS, https://www.meshy.ai/pricing. Confidence: high (licenses), medium (quality; the test was about product visualization, not games).

**H6. Stylization.** For Godot there are ready-made PSX shaders (vertex snapping, affine textures, dithering): PSX Look Free, Ultimate Retro Shader Collection, godot-psx-style-demo (from an Asset Library and GitHub snippet); 4.7 added nearest-neighbor scaling. Low-poly and PSX lower the requirements for retopology, textures, and style consistency of AI assets (my conclusion). Confidence: medium.

**H7. License of the source assets.** The tgstation README: all assets, including icons and sound, are under CC BY-SA 3.0 unless otherwise noted; the code is under AGPL v3. 3D models built from sprites (tracing, voxelization, AI from an image) will probably be derivative works and fall under ShareAlike. A lawyer's assessment is needed; this direction is outside my task. Confidence: high (README text), low (interpretation).

## Implications for the 3D port

1. **Separate the rules from the picture.** An entity's position in the simulation is a tile plus a sub-tile offset; the 3D scene is a representation. Compute visibility, audibility, and FOV on the server (the analog of view/hearers), not in the renderer, so that a camera change does not break the rules.
2. **Choose the camera before the art.** The baseline option with the minimal risk for art and visibility: an angled rotating top-down camera (the SS3D path) with cutaway of walls and ceilings and a z-slice. First-person or TPS add: a narrow field of view, the need for a hand model, pressure on model quality at close range, spatial voice. Keep VR out of scope (SpessVR itself warns about motion sickness).
3. **2 m grid and free-placed items.** Build structures modularly at 2 m (this matches Godot GridMap, Stationeers, and the Quaternius kits); place items by points on surfaces or with physics; items slightly smaller than a tile.
4. **Archetypes instead of 11.4 thousand models.** Build a "typepath → mesh archetype + material + decal/icon" manifest with a script over DMI and typepaths; hand-draw only 50-150 recognizable items; the rest parametrically (crate shape, bottle, box, tool). Keep DMI icons for UI, posters, screens, and decals.
5. **A low-poly/PSX style at low resolution** keeps things uniform and lowers the cost; billboard sprites remain as a fallback for clutter and secondary items.
6. **Use AI generation as a draft** for blockouts to be retopologized by hand, after checking the licenses (Hunyuan3D is unavailable in the EU, the UK, and South Korea) and the provenance of the input images (CC BY-SA).
7. **Characters:** one humanoid skeleton, retargeting in Godot, CC0 animations (Quaternius UAL) as a base; clothing as meshes on the shared skeleton with hiding of body parts and shape keys for hairstyles; reduce the 1.8 thousand clothing types to a dozen or two shapes plus color and decal variants; held items are rigid, with attachment points and 5-10 poses, and only clothing gets a rigged model (this saves the second model from SS3D practice).
8. **Lighting:** bake the structural light, leave dynamic lights to emergency lighting, flashlights, and fire; no more shadow-casting sources than the default atlas allows (about 88); a minimal ambient and a brightness setting; baked light conflicts with destructible walls (then VoxelGI/SDFGI).
9. **Atmospherics:** grid cells, an algorithm based on the pressure gradient (Stationeers experience; ⚠️ "separate threads" here is my recommendation, not a confirmed Stationeers practice); visualization (vacuum, fire, smoke) through particles and fog volumes, rather than rendering "gas" per tile.
10. **Interaction:** a single click model (raycast by entity-id, selection by ID color or physics), a contextual radial menu, two hands, intent as a modifier; a limited set of interaction archetypes (button, lever, container, door, item, machine with UI); external data and scripts.
11. **Sound:** reuse about 3.8 thousand audio files (ogg/mp3; ⚠️ 4423 is all the files of the sound directory, including 531 .mid and auxiliary files); 3D attenuation, occlusion via raycasts, reverberation by room, muffling when pressure drops.
12. **Process:** the first milestone is a vertical slice of 1-2 rooms with 10-20 interactions and 2 players, not "the whole station". The history of SS3D, Primulus, Centration, and Hellion shows that scope and team retention kill projects more often than technology. ⚠️ This is an interpretation, not an established fact: according to the verified sources the causes differ (Primulus: the Kickstarter failure; Centration: the wiki names supposed technical limitations of the engine; Hellion: few players, technical problems, and money; SS3D: volunteer turnover, while the bottlenecks per the devblog are animation and atmospherics). The study contains no comparative statistics of "scope versus technology"; the conclusion "start with a vertical slice" does not depend on that and remains reasonable.

## Open questions (for the user)

1. Camera: angled top-down with rotation, first-person, a hybrid, or in-game switching of views? Is VR needed?
2. Goal: parity with SS13 mechanics, the "SS13 feel" (like SpessVR), or a new inspired-by game? The number of objects depends on this.
3. Style: low-poly/PSX, HD-2D with billboard sprites, voxels, or something more realistic?
4. Scale: are a 2 m cell and a ceiling of about 3 m suitable? Is free (non-tile) construction needed?
5. Z-levels: are they mandatory for the MVP (IceBox, Tram)?
6. Zero gravity and EVA: keep the simplified SS13 model or build true 3D movement?
7. Team and budget: who makes the art? Is AI generation with manual refinement acceptable? What jurisdiction are you in (Hunyuan3D license)? Is it acceptable to release 3D assets under CC BY-SA?
8. Radio and voice: keep the text radio or add proximity voice?
9. Darkness: are flashlights and a user brightness setting acceptable?
10. Which SS13 systems are "untouchable" (atmospherics, chemistry, surgery, construction)? This determines the art and the interfaces.
